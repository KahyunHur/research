# Multilayered separators with core-shell structured nanocellulose-SiO2 nanocomposites for lithium-ion batteries

**Authors:** Kim, Hyeyun; Lee, Chaeeun; Jo, Jaemin; Yu, Seonmyeong; Shin, Sunghee; Hur, Kahyun; Koo, Bonwook; Kim, Kwang Ho; Hwang, Jinyeon
**Journal:** Carbohydrate Polymers
**Year:** 2025
**Volume:** 362
**Pages:** 123677
**Link:** [DOI: 10.1016/j.carbpol.2025.123677](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.carbpol.2025.123677)

## Abstract / Summary
Multilayered porous separators consisting of cellulose nanofibers (CNF) and SiO2 coating are fabricated for lithium-ion batteries (LIBs) as an eco-friendly alternative to conventional polyolefin separators. Employing a sol-gel synthesis method, SiO2 nanoparticles are intricately arranged on CNF to create core-shell structured CNF-SiO2 composites. Simple binder-free CNF-SiO2 surface coated composite separators are obtained via alternating sequential vacuum filtration of CNF suspensions and the nanocomposite coating functional layers, resulting in bi- and tri-layered separators. CNF entangled structure determines the pore architecture of CNF-SiO2 as a molecular template, while simultaneously tailoring the size distribution of pores and fibers within the separator, thus optimizing Li-ion transport pathways. By combining core-shell structured CNF-SiO2 nanocomposites as a functional layer with CNF separators, the resulting multilayer separators significantly improve the electrochemical stability of LIBs due to the effective suppression of electrolyte decomposition and dendrite growth on the Li metal surface. This approach simplifies material sourcing and production processes, making it particularly attractive for large-scale manufacturing for LIBs separators from carbohydrate precursors extracted from biomass. This study highlights the potential of chemically modified cellulose-based nanostructures as high-performing upcycled separators for energy storage, resulting in their possible commercial applications.


## Layman's Summary
**The Concept:** 
Imagine a battery separator made not from petroleum-based plastic, but from highly refined wood fibers and tiny glass-like particles. This research introduces a "core-shell" sandwich design that uses plant-derived materials to make [[Lithium Batteries|lithium-ion batteries]] greener, safer, and longer-lasting.

**The Problem:** 
Modern batteries rely on thin plastic sheets to keep the positive and negative sides apart. These plastics are made from oil and can melt if the battery gets too hot, leading to dangerous fires. Scientists want to use [[Cellulose|cellulose]] (the structural material in plants) because it handles heat much better and is eco-friendly. However, cellulose naturally "likes" water. Any trapped moisture inside a battery causes harmful chemical reactions that destroy the battery's performance over time.

**The Solution:** 
The research team developed a clever way to wrap each tiny fiber in a protective shield to keep it "dry" and stable:
*   **Direct Shielding:** They used a chemical process to grow a shell of silica (the main ingredient in sand) directly onto [[Cellulose|cellulose nanofibers]]. 
*   **Binder-Free Design:** Instead of using chemical glues (binders) that can create unwanted gas or gunk, they filtered the fibers so they naturally locked together into a sturdy sheet.
*   **Multi-Layering:** They stacked these protected fibers in layers to create a sophisticated "filter" that controls how energy moves through the battery.

**Why This is Special:** 
*   **Superior Heat Resistance:** These separators can withstand temperatures well above 200°C, whereas standard plastic separators melt and fail.
*   **Dendrite Blocking:** The tough silica-coated structure stops "dendrites"—sharp lithium spikes that grow like weeds inside batteries—from poking through and causing short circuits.
*   **Sustainable Sourcing:** The materials are made from biomass, supporting "carbon neutrality" and reducing our reliance on fossil fuels.
*   **Precision Engineering:** By using the fibers as a template, the team created these [[Nanomaterials|nanocomposites]] with perfectly sized pores, allowing energy to flow efficiently while blocking damage.

**The Takeaway:** 
This study shows that we don't have to choose between "green" and "high-performance." By using upcycled plant materials protected by a high-tech mineral shell, we can build the next generation of safe, high-capacity batteries for everything from smartphones to electric vehicles.
