# Programmable In-Situ Interactions Between Resins and Photopolymerized Structures for Seamlessly Integrated Optical Manufacturing of Microlenses

**Authors:** Kim, Kibeom; Oh, Sangmin; Hur, Kahyun; Cho, Hyesung
**Journal:** Advanced Functional Materials
**Year:** 2026
**Volume:** 36
**Pages:** e28056
**Link:** [DOI: 10.1002/adfm.202528056](https://doi.org/10.1002/adfm.202528056)

![[2026AFM - Programmable In-Situ Interactions Between Resins and Photopolymerized Structures for Seamlessly Integrated Optical Manufacturing of Microlenses_infographic.jpg]]

## Abstract / Summary
Photopolymerization is typically used to directly create solid structures by controlling light irradiation onto liquid resins. However, this approach lacks the ability to accommodate three-phase interactions within the resin, which could otherwise be harnessed to carve curvilinear structures in a programmable manner. Here, we present an opposite strategy that induces resin interactions by embedding in situ photopolymerized confinement, enabling a highly integrated and seamless optical workflow for the manufacturing, characterization, and qualification of microlenses. In our demonstration, spatiotemporally controlled, multiplexed ultraviolet exposure locally solidifies photocurable resins outside the regions of interest, leading to the spontaneous development of concave curvatures in the unexposed liquid domains through programmable three-phase interfaces. This curvature evolution is quantitatively tracked in real time via in situ pinhole image analysis and can be fixed by short light exposure to preserve the targeted lens geometry. Furthermore, we present an intensity-compensation algorithm to resolve spatial non-uniformities in optical writing setups, thereby yielding microlens arrays with high uniformity. Finally, we demonstrate in situ qualification of microlenses across different fabrication batches, where pinhole images are quantitatively analyzed and tracked to assess batch-to-batch consistency. Our seamlessly optically integrated workflow with in situ interactions would be helpful in designing setups for other optical components toward curvilinear structures, in situ photopolymerization, microlens arrays, optically integrated processes and advanced applications.


## Layman's Summary
**The Concept:**
Imagine trying to create a tiny, perfect drop of water that stays exactly where you want it and acts like a magnifying glass. This is the goal of creating microlenses—tiny lenses used in everything from smartphone cameras to virtual reality (VR) headsets. The vision here is to use light like a "sculptor" to build a solid frame around liquid resin, allowing the liquid to naturally pull itself into a perfect, smooth curve to create a high-quality lens.

**The Problem:**
Making these tiny lenses is usually a trade-off. You can use [[2D_3D_4D Printing|3D printing]] to "write" a lens shape, but it often leaves tiny bumps or layers that blur the image. Alternatively, you can use liquid droplets that are perfectly smooth, but it is very hard to make thousands of them exactly the same size and in the right place. Furthermore, you usually can't tell if the lenses are good until the whole manufacturing process is finished and you take them to a different machine to test them.

**The Solution:**
The research team at KIST developed a "seamless" workflow that builds, watches, and tests the lenses all at once:
1.  **Light-Fencing:** They use UV light to instantly harden a "fence" of resin around a liquid center. 
2.  **Natural Curving:** Instead of forcing the liquid into a shape, they let the physics of [[Self-assembly|self-assembly]] take over. The liquid inside the "fence" naturally curves into a smooth, concave shape.
3.  **Real-Time Watching:** They use a smart camera system to watch how light passes through the lens while it is still forming.
4.  **The Final Snap:** Once the curve is perfect, they hit it with a final burst of light to "freeze" the liquid into a solid, permanent lens.
5.  **Smart Correction:** They use a special computer algorithm to ensure every lens in a large grid is identical, even if the light source isn't perfectly even.

**Why This is Special:**
*   **Mirror-Smooth Finish:** Because the lens surface is formed by liquid tension rather than being built layer-by-layer, it is incredibly smooth, which means clearer images.
*   **All-in-One Factory:** The system manufactures, measures, and checks the quality of the lenses in one single step, saving time and reducing errors.
*   **Perfect Uniformity:** Using their intensity-compensation algorithm, they can create massive arrays of lenses where every single one performs exactly the same.
*   **Programmable Shapes:** By changing how they shine the UV light, they can "program" the liquid to form different types of curves for different optical needs.

**The Takeaway:**
This new method turns the manufacturing of advanced optics into a smart, self-correcting process. It paves the way for better and cheaper [[Sensors|optical sensors]], more realistic VR displays, and even tinier medical cameras by combining the precision of light-based [[Polymerization|photopolymerization]] with the natural perfection of liquid surfaces.
