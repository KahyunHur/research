# Optically Switchable Smart Windows with Integrated Photovoltaic Devices

**Authors:** Kwon, Hyun-Keun; Lee, Kyu-Tae; Hur, Kahyun; Moon, Sung Hwan; Quasim, Malik M.; Wilkinson, Timothy D.; Han, Ji-Young; Ko, Hyungduk; Han, Il-Ki; Park, Byoungnam; Min, Byoung Koun; Ju, Byeong-Kwon; Morris, Stephen M.; Friend, Richard H.; Ko, Doo-Hyun
**Journal:** Advanced Energy Materials
**Year:** 2015
**Volume:** 5
**Pages:** 1401347
**Link:** [DOI: 10.1002/aenm.201401347](https://doi.org/10.1002/aenm.201401347)

![[2015AdvEnerMat - Optically Switchable Smart Windows with Integrated Photovoltaic Devices_infographic.jpg]]

## Abstract / Summary
An optically controlled energy-harvesting smart window that incorporates a semitransparent solar cell with a photo­sensitive liquid crystal (LC) layer is demonstrated. The LC layer can switch between a transparent (day mode) and an opaque (night mode) state depending upon the incident solar radiation. Combined with a photovoltaic cell, this window provides a template for future smart window systems.


## Layman's Summary
**The Concept:**
Imagine a window that acts like a giant pair of "transition lenses" for your home, but with a high-tech twist: it generates electricity while you look through it. This research introduces a "smart window" that stays clear and harvests solar energy during the day, then automatically turns opaque at night to act as a private shutter—all without you ever having to flip a switch or plug it into a wall.

**The Problem:**
Most modern smart windows are "electrochromic," which means they require a constant supply of external electricity to change from clear to dark. This is counter-intuitive if the goal is to save energy. Furthermore, windows represent a massive amount of surface area on buildings that usually just lets light pass through without capturing any of that energy for use.

**The Solution:**
The research team developed a clever "sandwich" design that combines two main components:
*   **An Optically Tunable Layer:** This uses [[Liquid Crystal|liquid crystals]] mixed with special light-sensitive molecules. These molecules change their shape when hit by UV light (sunlight), causing the window to become transparent. When the sun goes down, they flip back to their original shape, making the window dark and opaque.
*   **Integrated Solar Cells:** A transparent solar cell is layered into the window. It captures sunlight to generate power while still being clear enough to see through.
*   **Zero-Power Switching:** Because the window reacts directly to the presence or absence of UV light, it doesn't need an external battery or power source to change its transparency.

**Why This is Special:**
*   **Two-in-One Utility:** It serves as both a functional window shutter for privacy and a clean energy generator.
*   **Automatic Privacy:** The window knows when it is "night mode" based on the light levels, automatically shielding your home from outside view.
*   **Computational Design:** The scientists used advanced [[DFT|Density Functional Theory (DFT)]]—a type of complex computer modeling—to perfectly tune the chemical structure of the molecules so they react quickly and stay stable over time.
*   **Sustainability:** By using [[Sustainable Polymers|photoresponsive materials]], the device promotes energy-positive buildings that can actually help power themselves.

**The Takeaway:**
This technology turns every window in a skyscraper or home into a self-operating power plant. By combining energy harvesting with automatic light control, we can create buildings that are not only more private and comfortable but also significantly more eco-friendly and energy-independent.
