## Abstract / Summary
The growing demand for high-energy-density, safe, and sustainable lithium-ion batteries (LIBs) necessitates the development of innovative electrolytes. Herein, we present a facile in situ preparation strategy for fabricating a high-performance single-ion conductor (SC). This SC is based on hydroxypropyl cellulose (HPC) integrated with polyethylene glycol diacrylate cross-linker, in combination with sodium styrene sulfonate (NaSS) as a functional monomer. The introduction of NaSS is crucial, as it introduces sulfonate groups that are immobilized within the polymer network, enabling selective lithium-ion transport. This approach offers a significant advancement over conventional polyether-based gel polymer electrolytes (GPEs), which usually suffer from limited oxidative stability and require the use of separators, particularly in high-voltage battery applications. The in situ polymerization method presented here eliminates the need for a separator and offers several key advantages: rapid processability, excellent scalability, and the formation of a stable solid electrolyte interface. The result is a robust, separator-free GPE. Our HPC-based single-ion conductor (MHPC SC) exhibits a high lithium transference number of 0.89 and an ionic conductivity of 2.4 mS cm–1 at room temperature. These properties are attributed to efficient lithium-ion transport through its synergistic effect of the polyanionic conductor and HPC matrix. The mechanically robust and highly conformal polymer network effectively suppresses detrimental interfacial reactions and mitigates dendrite growth, resulting in an enhanced cycling stability. Notably, the MHPC SC enables stable operation with high-voltage cathodes up to 4.3 V, achieving 94% capacity retention over 100 cycles. These findings highlight the potential of cellulose-based GPEs as a sustainable and high-performance electrolyte that significantly enhances both the safety and performance of advanced LIBs.


## Layman's Summary
**The Concept:**
Our modern world runs on [[Lithium Batteries|lithium-ion batteries]], powering everything from cell phones to electric cars. As our technology advances, we constantly need batteries that can pack more energy, last longer, and, most importantly, operate safely without the risk of overheating or catching fire.

**The Problem:**
Traditional batteries use liquid fluids (electrolytes) to move energy around, but these liquids can leak, break down, or easily catch fire. Scientists have tried replacing these hazardous liquids with safer, jelly-like materials called [[Polymer Electrolytes|polymer electrolytes]]. However, these jellies are often physically weak, struggle to handle high electrical voltages, and require a bulky plastic divider (a separator) to prevent short circuits, which ends up wasting valuable space inside the battery.

**The Solution:**
To fix this, the research team developed a new type of battery gel using a surprising, nature-inspired ingredient: [[Cellulose]], a sustainable material found in plant cell walls. By mixing modified cellulose with specific chemical linkers and exposing it to light, they can instantly solidify the gel directly inside the battery—a rapid process known as light-based [[Polymerization]]. This creates a custom-fit, solid jelly that perfectly hugs the inside of the battery components.

**Why This is Special:**
* **No Extra Parts Needed:** The cellulose jelly is so physically strong that it completely eliminates the need for a traditional plastic divider, freeing up space to pack in more energy.
* **One-Way Traffic:** It acts as a "single-ion conductor," meaning it expertly directs only the necessary lithium particles where they need to go, making the battery run much more efficiently.
* **Stops Dangerous Spikes:** The tough gel acts as a physical shield, stopping harmful metal spikes (called dendrites) from growing inside and ruining the battery.
* **Handles High Power:** Unlike older jellies, this new material remains highly stable even when connected to high-voltage battery parts.
* **Eco-Friendly Edge:** Using cellulose makes the battery manufacturing process more sustainable and environmentally friendly.

**The Takeaway:**
By replacing hazardous liquids with a tough, plant-inspired jelly, this research offers a practical recipe for the next generation of energy storage. It brings us one step closer to ultra-safe, high-capacity batteries that could reliably power our future devices and electric vehicles for years to come.
