# Additive Manufacturing

**Key Focus Areas / Related Concepts**: [[2D_3D_4D Printing|2D/3D/4D printing]]

See also our [[Journal Publications]] for more details on our findings related to Additive Manufacturing.

## Overview
Additive manufacturing is like building a house brick by brick, rather than carving it out of a single large block of stone. Instead of cutting material away, this method builds objects layer by layer from digital blueprints. This approach significantly reduces waste and allows researchers to create incredibly complex shapes and intricate internal structures that would be impossible to achieve with traditional, subtractive manufacturing methods.

In our lab, we push the boundaries of this technology through advanced [[2D_3D_4D Printing|2D/3D/4D printing]]. While traditional 3D printing creates static objects, 4D printing adds the dimension of time, meaning the printed structures can actually change shape, move, or adapt in response to their environment—such as a shift in temperature, light, or moisture. By seamlessly integrating these techniques, we can embed ultra-thin sensors and responsive [[Smart Materials|smart materials]] directly into devices as they are being constructed layer by layer.

This revolutionary approach is transforming how we design and build future technologies. By mastering these multidimensional printing techniques, we are able to rapidly prototype dynamic systems ranging from self-assembling robotics to custom components for next-generation [[Sustainable Batteries|sustainable batteries]]. Ultimately, it allows us to bridge the gap between static hardware and dynamic, responsive systems that adapt to real-world needs.
