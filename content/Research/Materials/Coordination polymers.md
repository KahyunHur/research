# Coordination polymers

This is a key research area in our lab under the **Materials** division.

**Key Focus Areas / Related Concepts**: [[Halide-bridged Coordination Polymer]]

See also our [[Journal Publications]] for more details on our findings related to Coordination polymers.

## Overview
Coordination polymers are like molecular LEGO sets where metal atoms act as the structural joints, held together by chemical connectors into vast, repeating networks. By carefully selecting these components, scientists can "tune" a material's properties, creating structures that can capture gases, sense chemicals, or conduct electricity. They represent a frontier in materials science where we design functions from the bottom up, atom by atom, to solve complex engineering challenges.

Our lab specializes in a specific variety known as the [[Halide-bridged Coordination Polymer]]. In these systems, halides—such as bromine or iodine—serve as the critical links between metal centers. This specific architecture is vital for [[Neuromorphic Computing]], an area of research aimed at building hardware that mimics the energy efficiency and learning structure of the human brain rather than relying on traditional, power-hungry processors.

By utilizing these halide-bridged frameworks, we develop advanced [[Electrolyte-gated Synapse]] devices. These components allow for the precise movement of ions, enabling electronic "synapses" that can learn and adapt just like biological ones. This work is essential for creating Physical AI systems that are faster and more capable of complex processing by using materials that inherently "think" more like we do.
