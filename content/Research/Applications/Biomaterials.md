# Biomaterials

This is a key research area in our lab under the **Applications** division.

**Key Focus Areas / Related Concepts**: [[Nucleic acid and protein engineering]]

See also our [[Journal Publications]] for more details on our findings related to Biomaterials.

## Overview
[[Biomaterials]] are engineered substances designed to interact safely and effectively with biological systems for medical purposes. Traditionally, these materials were used primarily for structural roles, such as joint replacements or dental implants. Today, the field has evolved dramatically, focusing on creating dynamic, interactive materials that can communicate directly with the body's own cells and molecular machinery to actively promote healing and treat complex conditions.

Our lab's specific focus is on [[Nucleic acid and protein engineering]], which allows us to build these advanced materials using the fundamental building blocks of life. By carefully redesigning DNA, RNA, and proteins, we can program these molecules to perform highly specific tasks within the body. For example, we can engineer proteins to act as targeted delivery vehicles that carry medicine exclusively to diseased cells, or we can design nucleic acid structures that provide a supportive scaffold to help regenerate damaged tissues.

This approach is incredibly important because it provides unprecedented precision in modern healthcare. Instead of relying on broad treatments that can cause systemic side effects, our engineered biological molecules can deliver therapies exactly where and when they are needed. By combining the principles of biomaterials with the highly programmable nature of these molecules, we are paving the way for next-generation [[nanomedicine]] and highly personalized therapeutic solutions.
