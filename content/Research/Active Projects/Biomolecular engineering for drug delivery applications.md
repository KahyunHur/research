## Overview
[[Biomolecular Engineering|Biomolecular engineering]] involves designing and modifying biological molecules—like proteins or DNA—to perform specific, highly targeted tasks. In our lab, we focus on using these biological building blocks to create advanced systems for getting medicine exactly where it needs to go in the body. Imagine a microscopic delivery truck that only stops at diseased cells, completely ignoring the healthy ones; that is the core promise of this technology.

From a [[Materials Science|materials science]] perspective, we treat these complex biomolecules as versatile raw materials rather than just biological components. By carefully controlling their physical and chemical properties, we can construct highly precise [[Drug Delivery|drug delivery]] vehicles, such as specialized nano-capsules or smart hydrogels. These lab-designed materials are engineered to protect fragile medicines from degrading in the bloodstream and to release their therapeutic payload only when triggered by specific conditions, like the unique environment surrounding a tumor.

This powerful intersection of biology and materials engineering is crucial for the future of medicine. By creating smarter, structurally sound materials, it allows us to develop safer, more effective treatments with significantly fewer side effects, transforming how we approach everything from targeted cancer therapies to next-generation vaccines.
