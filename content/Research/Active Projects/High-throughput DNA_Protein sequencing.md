## Overview
Imagine trying to read a library of billions of books in just a few days. That is the challenge of [[High-throughput Sequencing|High-throughput sequencing]], a revolutionary technology that allows scientists to rapidly "read" the genetic instructions hidden within DNA and proteins. Instead of decoding one letter at a time, this technology processes millions of genetic fragments simultaneously. This massive scale of information gathering has completely transformed medicine and biology, enabling personalized treatments and rapid disease detection.

While it sounds like purely biological research, the secret to making this technology faster and cheaper lies in materials science. Our lab focuses on designing the cutting-edge physical components that make modern sequencing possible. We engineer specialized [[Biosensors|biosensors]] and ultra-thin [[Nanomaterials|nanomaterials]] that can accurately detect the microscopic electrical or optical signals produced when a single molecule of DNA or protein passes by. 

By discovering and refining new materials for these devices, we are helping to build the next generation of sequencing tools. Whether it is creating more durable membranes for single-molecule reading or developing surfaces that interact perfectly with biological samples, our materials research directly powers the life-saving biological breakthroughs of tomorrow.
