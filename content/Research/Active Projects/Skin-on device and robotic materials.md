# Skin-on device and robotic materials

See also our [[Journal Publications]] for more details on our findings related to Skin-on device and robotic materials.

## Overview
Skin-on devices represent a new frontier in wearable technology, consisting of ultra-thin, flexible electronics that adhere directly to the human body. These systems are designed to monitor physiological signals or provide tactile feedback without the bulk of traditional hardware. Our lab approaches this by exploring the fundamental properties of [[Biocompatible Materials]] that can stretch and bend just like human tissue while maintaining high electrical performance.

Beyond wearables, we are integrating these capabilities into the fabric of machines through the development of robotic materials. By embedding sensing and processing directly into a robot's "skin," we move closer to the realization of [[Physical AI|Physical AI]], where the body itself contributes to intelligent behavior. This allows robots to safely interact with humans and adapt to complex, unpredictable environments in real-time.

To accelerate these breakthroughs, we utilize [[Self-Driving Labs (SDL)|Self-Driving Labs (SDL)]] to automate the discovery of novel composites. This approach enables us to precisely tune the mechanical and electronic properties of materials, ensuring that future [[Soft Robotics]] are not only functional but also resilient enough for everyday use.
