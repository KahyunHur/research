## Overview
Everything around us emits heat in the form of invisible [[Infrared Radiation|infrared radiation]]. By designing advanced materials that can control how this light is absorbed, reflected, or emitted, we can create surfaces with remarkable thermal abilities. As materials scientists, our research focuses on engineering these unique structures at the microscopic level, allowing us to manipulate heat flow in ways that traditional materials simply cannot.

One major application of this technology is [[Radiative Cooling|radiative cooling]]. This process allows a surface to cool itself below the ambient air temperature without using any electricity. By tailoring materials to emit heat specifically into the cold expanse of outer space while reflecting the sun's energy, we can significantly reduce the power needed for air conditioning in buildings. We are constantly exploring new [[Polymers|polymers]] and nanoscale coatings that can make these cooling panels more efficient, durable, and cost-effective for everyday use.

Another fascinating use for infrared control is in [[Thermal Camouflage|thermal camouflage]]. Because infrared cameras detect the heat signatures of objects and people, materials that can dynamically change their heat emission can effectively hide an object from thermal detection. By developing innovative, responsive materials that can tune their infrared properties on demand, our lab is paving the way for advanced smart fabrics that adapt to environmental changes, providing both strategic concealment and personalized temperature regulation.
