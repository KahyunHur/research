# Polymer electrolytes for lithium batteries

This is a key research area in our lab under the **Active Projects** division.

See also our [[Journal Publications]] for more details on our findings related to Polymer electrolytes for lithium batteries.

## Overview
Traditional [[lithium batteries]], the kind that power everything from our smartphones to electric vehicles, typically rely on liquid chemicals to move energy back and forth. While effective, these liquids can be flammable and prone to leaking, raising significant safety concerns. To solve this, our lab is focusing on replacing these volatile liquids with solid [[polymer electrolytes]]. These are flexible, plastic-like materials that can safely conduct electricity within the battery. By shifting to a solid state, we eliminate the risk of leaks and significantly reduce the chance of battery fires, paving the way for safer everyday electronics.

From a [[materials science]] perspective, designing the perfect polymer for this job is a delicate balancing act. The material needs to be physically robust enough to prevent internal short circuits, yet flexible enough at the microscopic level to allow [[lithium ions]] to flow freely. We are actively developing and testing new molecular structures that improve this flow without compromising safety or durability. Ultimately, this research aims to unlock a new generation of energy storage that is not only inherently safer but can also pack more power into a smaller, lighter form factor.
