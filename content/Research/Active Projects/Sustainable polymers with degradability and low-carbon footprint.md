# Sustainable polymers with degradability and low-carbon footprint

This is a key research area in our lab under the **Active Projects** division.

See also our [[Journal Publications]] for more details on our findings related to Sustainable polymers with degradability and low-carbon footprint.

## Overview
Traditional plastics and polymers are often derived from petroleum, contributing to a high carbon footprint and persisting in the environment for centuries. Our research aims to replace these conventional materials with sustainable alternatives that feature built-in [[degradability]]. By designing polymers that can break down naturally at the end of their life cycle, we can significantly reduce plastic waste and minimize the long-term ecological impact of the products we use every day.

In the context of our lab's work on energy storage, these innovations are driving the development of [[Sustainable Batteries]]. We are currently focused on creating [[Cellulose-based binders]] and [[fluorine-free]] electrolytes that maintain high performance without the environmental risks associated with traditional battery chemistries. This transition ensures that as we move toward a renewable energy economy, the materials at the heart of our technology are as sustainable as the power they store.
