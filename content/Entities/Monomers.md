# Monomers

## What is it?
The word "monomer" means "single part" (*mono* = one, *mer* = part). A **Monomer** is a small molecule that can be chemically bonded to other identical molecules to form a giant, repeating chain called a **[[Polymers|polymer]]**. If a polymer is like a long pearl necklace, then each individual pearl is a monomer. Or, if you think of a polymer as a long train, each individual train car is a monomer.

## Why is it Important?
Monomers are the fundamental building blocks of almost every material in our modern world. By carefully choosing the right monomer, scientists can "program" the final material to be soft and rubbery, hard and clear, or even conductive like a metal. 

In our research, we focus on:
- **Custom Synthesis:** Designing new monomers with specific side-chains, such as the methacrylates used in **HPC-MA**, to give cellulose-based materials the ability to cross-link and become stable gels.
- **[[Polymerization]]:** Studying the chemical reactions that hook these monomers together. Controlling how fast and in what order they connect allows us to create advanced **[[Block Copolymer]]** structures and **[[Smart Materials]]**.
- **[[Degradability]]:** Designing "green" monomers that can be easily unhooked from each other by nature, ensuring that the final plastics don't turn into permanent **[[Microplastics]]** but instead return safely to the environment.
