# Lithium batteries

Lithium batteries are an important application in our research. 

Related to: [[Energy applications]]

## What is it?
Imagine a "rocking chair" for energy. In a lithium battery, Lithium ions act like tiny, energetic passengers that move back and forth between two sides of the chair: the Anode (the negative side) and the Cathode (the positive side). When you plug your phone into a charger, you are essentially pushing all those lithium ions into the Anode, where they are stored like a tightly coiled spring. As you use your device, the spring is released, and the ions "rock" back over to the Cathode through a liquid medium called an Electrolyte, creating a flow of electricity that powers your screen and apps.

## Why is it Important?
Lithium batteries are the "heartbeat" of the modern digital and mobile revolution. They are uniquely valuable because they possess a very high Energy Density, meaning they can store a massive amount of power in a remarkably small and lightweight package. This efficiency is what allows us to have slim smartphones, powerful laptops, and high-performance Electric Vehicles (EVs) that can travel hundreds of miles on a single charge. Without this high-capacity Energy Storage technology, our portable devices would be significantly bulkier and require much more frequent tethering to a wall outlet.

Beyond personal gadgets, these batteries are a cornerstone of the global effort toward Decarbonization. They provide a critical solution to the "intermittency" problem of Renewable Energy sources; because the sun doesn't always shine and the wind doesn't always blow, we need large-scale Battery Storage to hold onto that green energy for later use. By enabling a more reliable and efficient Clean Energy grid, lithium batteries are helping to reduce our reliance on fossil fuels and are central to the technological fight against Climate Change.
