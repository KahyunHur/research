# Neuromorphic Device

## What is it?
A **Neuromorphic Device** is a specialized electronic component—like a transistor or a resistor—that is designed to behave like a biological brain cell (neuron) or the connection between cells (synapse). Unlike a standard computer chip that only understands "on" and "off" (0 and 1), a neuromorphic device can have many different states in between. It can "remember" its past history of electrical pulses, becoming stronger or weaker over time. This property, called **Plasticity**, is the physical basis of learning.

## Why is it Important?
Neuromorphic devices are the physical building blocks of **[[Neuromorphic Computing]]**. They allow us to move away from bulky, energy-hungry software simulations of neural networks toward sleek, ultra-low-power hardware. 

Key technologies we research include:
- **Three-terminal Artificial Synapse:** These devices use a third "gate" terminal to independently control the synaptic weight, providing much better stability and linearity than simpler two-terminal devices.
- **Memristor:** A "memory resistor" that changes its resistance based on the history of voltage applied to it.
- **MIEC Materials:** Using Mixed Ionic-Electronic Conductors (like CuBr2) to create devices where the movement of ions (like in the brain) and electrons (like in a computer) are seamlessly integrated. 
By perfecting these devices, we can create [[Skin-on Device]] sensors and [[Physical AI]] systems that are as efficient and adaptable as the human nervous system.
