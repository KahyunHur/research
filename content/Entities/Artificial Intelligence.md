# Artificial Intelligence

## What is it?
In the context of [[Materials Science]], **Artificial Intelligence** (AI) is like having a super-powered research assistant that can read millions of scientific papers, predict the results of experiments before they happen, and even suggest entirely new molecules that no human has thought of yet. Unlike traditional software that follows rigid rules, AI "learns" from vast amounts of data to identify complex patterns and make intelligent decisions.

## Why is it Important?
AI is fundamentally accelerating the speed of discovery. Traditionally, finding a new material for a battery could take a decade of trial-and-error. With AI-driven tools like MatterGen, we can use "Generative AI" to design thousands of candidate structures in minutes. 

Key applications in our research include:
- **[[Self-Driving Labs (SDL)]]:** Using AI to control robotic arms and sensors to run experiments 24/7 without human intervention.
- **Property Prediction:** Using MLIP and equivariant neural networks to simulate how atoms interact with nearly the same accuracy as a supercomputer but at a fraction of the speed.
- **Inverse Design:** Telling the AI exactly what properties we want (e.g., "I need a material that is 5V stable and highly conductive") and having the AI work backward to find the chemical formula that matches. This synergy between AI and physical systems is the foundation of [[Physical AI]].
