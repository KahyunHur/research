# Neuromorphic Computing

## What is it?
**Neuromorphic Computing** is a revolutionary approach to designing computers that mimic the structure and function of the human brain. Traditional computers separate "thinking" (the processor) from "memory" (the hard drive), which wastes a massive amount of energy moving data back and forth. The human brain, however, is thousands of times more efficient because its memory and processing are integrated into the same unit: the synapse. Neuromorphic systems use specialized hardware, like [[Memristor|memristors]], to build artificial neurons and synapses that can process information just like a living brain.

## Why is it Important?
This technology is essential for the future of **[[Artificial Intelligence]]** and the internet of things (IoT). Because neuromorphic chips are incredibly energy-efficient, they can enable "always-on" AI in tiny devices that run on a single battery for years. 

In our lab, we focus on:
- **In-sensor Computing:** Building sensors that can "think" and detect patterns (like a heartbeat anomaly) directly at the source, without sending data to a central computer.
- **[[Electrolyte-gated Synapse]]:** Using ion-conducting materials (like the lab's [[HPC-MA]]) to create artificial synapses that function using the same ionic signaling as biological systems.
- **Adaptive Learning:** Designing hardware that can learn and adapt to its environment in real-time, which is a fundamental requirement for [[Physical AI]] and autonomous robots. This shift from digital logic to brain-like "spike-based" processing is the key to achieving [[Intelligence per Joule]] levels that rival biology.
