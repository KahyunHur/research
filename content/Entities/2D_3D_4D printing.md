# 2D, 3D, and 4D Printing

## What is it?
Printing technology has evolved from simply putting ink on paper (2D) to building physical objects layer-by-layer (3D), and now to creating objects that can change over time (4D). 
- **2D Printing:** The traditional process of depositing a thin layer of material (like ink) on a flat surface. In advanced research, this includes DMD Maskless Lithography for creating microscopic circuit patterns.
- **3D Printing (Additive Manufacturing):** Building a three-dimensional object from a digital model by adding material (plastics, metals, or even [[Biomaterials]]) one layer at a time.
- **4D Printing:** The "fourth dimension" is time. 4D-printed objects are 3D-printed structures designed to transform, resize, or self-assemble when exposed to an external trigger like heat, light, or water. Imagine a flat sheet that folds itself into a box when placed in warm water.

## Why is it Important?
These technologies are fundamental to the "Lab-to-Fabrication" pipeline. 3D printing allows for rapid prototyping of complex structures like the Gyroid network or customized Microfluidic channels that would be impossible to make with traditional tools. 4D printing takes this further by enabling [[Smart Materials]] that can adapt to their environment. For instance, a 4D-printed [[Skin-on Device]] could adjust its shape to perfectly fit a moving joint, or a medical implant could expand to its final size only once it reaches the correct location inside the human body. This precision and adaptability are key to the future of [[Soft Robotics]] and autonomous Space Fab manufacturing.
