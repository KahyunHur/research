# Soft Robotics

## What is it?
Traditional robots are often made of rigid metal and hard plastic, which can be dangerous or clumsy when interacting with delicate objects or humans. **Soft Robotics** is a subfield of robotics that builds robots from highly flexible and compliant materials—like silicone, rubber, or advanced [[Polymers]]. Inspired by living organisms like octopuses or caterpillars, these robots can squeeze through tight spaces, adapt their shape to uneven terrain, and handle fragile objects (like a piece of fruit or a human heart) without causing damage.

## Why is it Important?
Soft robotics is merging the fields of biology and engineering to create machines that feel and move more like natural creatures. 

In our research, this involves:
- **[[Smart Materials]]:** Developing polymers that can change shape or stiffen on command when exposed to heat or light.
- **[[Skin-on Device]]:** Integrating flexible [[Sensors]] and [[Neuromorphic Computing]] chips directly onto soft robot bodies, giving them a "sense of touch."
- **Medical Applications:** Designing soft, biocompatible tools for minimally invasive surgery and targeted [[Drug Delivery]].
- **[[Physical AI]]:** Because soft robots are physically complex, we use AI to help them learn how to move and interact with their surroundings, a key goal for creating autonomous machines that can safely work alongside people in hospitals or homes.
