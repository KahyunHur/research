# Dipole

## What is it?
In simple terms, a **Dipole** occurs when there is a separation of positive and negative electrical charges within a single molecule or object. Think of it like a tiny molecular magnet with a "North" (positive) pole and a "South" (negative) pole. This charge separation creates an invisible force field—a dipole moment—that dictates how a molecule will orient itself and interact with its neighbors when an electric field is applied.

## Why is it Important?
Understanding dipoles is essential for designing materials that respond to electricity. In our research, dipoles play a central role in several areas:
- **[[Ferroelectric]] Materials:** In these materials, the dipoles can be switched back and forth by an external voltage, allowing us to store information (like the "0s" and "1s" in a computer) in the physical orientation of the molecules. This is a key mechanism for low-power [[Neuromorphic Device]] memory.
- **Solubility and Mixing:** Dipoles determine how different [[Polymers]] and solvents interact. We use tools like [[openCOSMO-RS]] to calculate these interactions, helping us predict if a new battery binder will dissolve or if a coolant in [[Immersion Cooling]] is compatible with the system's hardware.
- **[[Piezoelectric]] Sensors:** By arranging molecules with strong dipoles in specific patterns, we can create materials that generate electricity when they are squeezed or stretched, which is perfect for self-powered [[Skin-on Device]] sensors and [[Soft Robotics]].
