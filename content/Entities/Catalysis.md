# Catalysis

Catalysis is an important application in our research. 

Related to: [[Energy applications]]

## What is it?
Imagine you are trying to push a heavy boulder up a steep, exhausting hill; it takes a massive amount of time and energy to reach the other side. Now, picture someone revealing a secret tunnel that cuts straight through the mountain, allowing you to move the boulder to your destination with barely any effort. In chemistry, **catalysis** is the process of finding and using that shortcut. It involves adding a special substance called a [[catalyst]] to a [[chemical reaction]], which dramatically speeds up the process without being consumed or changed itself. 

By lowering the [[activation energy]]—the minimum "push" required to get a reaction started—catalysis allows these chemical transformations to happen much faster and often under less extreme conditions, like at lower temperatures. Once the reaction is complete, the catalyst emerges perfectly intact and ready to be used again, much like the secret tunnel remains open for the next traveler.

## Why is it Important?
Catalysis is the invisible engine driving both the natural world and modern industrial civilization. Within our own bodies, biological catalysts known as [[enzymes]] are absolutely essential for life. They rapidly break down food during [[metabolism]] and assist in copying [[DNA]]; without them, the chemical processes keeping us alive would happen too slowly to sustain human life.

In industry and technology, catalysis is equally revolutionary and foundational to modern society. It is the key to the [[Haber-Bosch process]], which produces the fertilizers required to sustain global food supplies, and it is a critical step in refining crude oil into usable fuels and [[polymers]]. As we move toward a more sustainable future, advanced fields like [[electrocatalysis]] and [[photocatalysis]] are unlocking clean energy technologies, making it possible to efficiently split water for hydrogen fuel cells and safely break down environmental pollutants.
